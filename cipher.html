The cipher table

26 letters, 26 two-letter codes. Most follow one rule — three don't, on purpose.

Marked cells are the exceptions Keane defined on purpose — K breaks the "next letter" pattern, L has its own dedicated code, and Z wraps back around instead of running off the alphabet. They're kept exactly as written, not smoothed out.

How the rule works

the short version
  • the pattern
    Every letter maps to itself followed by the next letter in the alphabet — A becomes "ab", B becomes "bc", and so on down the line.
  • exception: K
    By the pattern K should be "kl" — but L already has its own code, so K becomes "km" instead.
  • exception: L
    L breaks off from the sequence entirely and gets its own dedicated code: "lz".
  • exception: Z
    Z has no "next letter" to borrow from, so instead of running off the alphabet it wraps around to itself twice: "zz".
